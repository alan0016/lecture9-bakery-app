Home Bakery · Orders

A small app that helps a home baker take cupcake and cake orders from Instagram messages, keep the oven schedule within its hours, and track revenue and costs. Built for MGOC50 Lecture 9.

1. Start the app

  1. Open a terminal in this folder (the one with app.py).
  2. Type python app.py and press Enter. (On a Mac: python3 app.py.)
  3. Open http://127.0.0.1:8000 in your browser.
  4. To stop it, go back to the terminal and press Ctrl+C.

If it says port 8000 is busy, type python app.py 8001 and open http://127.0.0.1:8001 instead. Nothing needs to be installed: the app uses only Python's standard library.

2. Use it: one day at a time

The app plays the week of Monday 23 to Sunday 29 November 2026, one day at a time. It starts on Monday with the 13 orders taken before Monday (existing_orders.csv).

  1. Get the day's messages. Press Get the next day's messages at the top. The status line says Loading Monday's messages…, then Monday's messages loaded. The app reads that day's Instagram page (InstagramOrders_Monday.html and so on), message by message, in the order they were sent.
    • New orders go to Waiting for your decision. Each one says whether it fits the rules, and if not, which rule it breaks.
    • Changes and cancellations are applied automatically when they follow the rules. When they don't, they are refused, the order stays as it was, and the Messages list tells you why, so you can let the customer know.
  2. Decide.
    • Press Reject on any order you don't want.
    • If an order doesn't fit only because the oven is full, its row says so and tells you whether a helper would make it fit. Press Hire helper ($100) on that day in This week: the day gets 12 oven hours, and the order becomes ✓ Fits.
  3. Accept all and go to next day. This button asks you to confirm first. Then:
    • every waiting order that still fits is accepted, one by one, oldest first;
    • every order that still breaks a rule is rejected automatically;
    • today's accepted orders are marked picked up (5 PM), and the app moves to the next day.
    Then press Get the next day's messages again for the new day.

The app can't take actions back. To start the week over: stop the app (Ctrl+C), delete bakery.db, and start the app again. It rebuilds the file from existing_orders.csv, on Monday 23 November.

What the screen shows

  • Top bar: today's date, the Get the next day's messages button and a status line.
  • Waiting for your decision: one row per new order with the customer, product and quantity, pickup day, price and oven hours. It also shows the oven hours left on its pickup day, and ✓ Fits or the rule it breaks. Each row has a Reject button.
  • This week: one row per day.
    • A bar shows the oven hours used out of the day's limit (red = full).
    • Beside it are the day's orders and the day's revenue.
    • The Hire helper ($100) button is active only when the day is at least 2 days away.
  • Money: revenue from accepted orders, helper costs, and revenue minus helper costs.
  • Messages: every message, newest day first, tagged New order, Change applied / Change refused or Cancellation applied / Cancellation refused, with the reason.

3. The rules the app follows

From the "About the bakery" box on the Instagram pages and Lecture 9, slide 13. There is no AI in the app: every automatic decision is one of these fixed rules.

RuleHow the app applies it
Cupcakes $40 a dozen, 30 min in the oven; cake $120, 2 h. One product per order.Hours and price come from the product and quantity: 6 dozen = 3 h and $240.
The oven runs 8 hours a day; baking happens on the pickup day; pickup at 5 PM.A day's accepted orders can never add up to more than its hours. At the end of a day its orders become picked up.
A helper costs $100 and adds 4 oven hours to one day.Booking a helper sets that day's limit to 12 h and adds $100 to costs. A booked helper can't be cancelled in the app.
Orders, changes, cancellations and helper bookings need 2 days' notice. A message on Monday can be for Wednesday or later.Pickup day minus message day must be at least 2. Otherwise:
  • a new order is marked can't be taken;
  • a change or cancellation is refused and the order stays as it was;
  • the helper button is disabled.
The baker says yes or no to every new order.The app shows which new orders fit. You reject the ones you don't want. Accept all and go to next day (after you confirm) accepts the rest that fit and rejects the ones that still break a rule.

Messages are handled in the order they arrived. This matters on Tuesday: Brown cancels a Thursday order at 8:12 AM, which frees the hour that lets Reyes's 6 dozen fit Thursday exactly (5 + 3 = 8 h).

4. How it is built

One file, app.py, with three parts (Lecture 9, slide 17):

  • Front end: one page of plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript (the PAGE text in app.py). It asks the back end for the current state (GET /api/state) and sends each button press as one action (POST /api/…).
  • Back end: Python functions that check the rules and update the data. One action runs as one database transaction: if a rule is broken nothing changes, and the reason is shown at the top of the page.
  • Database: one SQLite file, bakery.db, created on the first start next to app.py.

Data model (slide 20)

Each fact is recorded once. An order's oven hours are saved with the order. A day's limit (8, or 12 with a helper) is saved with the day. The number of orders, hours used and left, and revenue of each day are not saved: the view day_totals counts them from the two tables every time, so they can never disagree.

TableColumnsOne row is
ordersid (order number), customer, product (cupcakes / cake), quantity, pickup (a day), hours (oven hours required), status, reason, received (message day)an order or a request
daysdate, total_hours (8, or 12 with a helper), helper (booked, $100: 0 or 1)a day; one day has many orders
day_totals (a view)date, total_hours, helper, helper_cost, orders (number of accepted orders), hours_used, hours_left, revenuea day with its totals, counted from days and orders
messagesday, time, customer, text, kind (order / change / cancel / unclear), outcome, result, order_idan Instagram message and what the app did with it
statekey, valuetoday's date, and which days' messages were loaded

Questions the tables answer:

  • How many oven hours are left on Friday? SELECT hours_left FROM day_totals WHERE date = '2026-11-27'
  • What is the total revenue this week? SELECT SUM(revenue) FROM day_totals WHERE date BETWEEN '2026-11-23' AND '2026-11-29'

State diagram of an order (slide 21)

Requested and Incompatible are both "waiting for your decision". An order moves between them when oven time frees up or fills (a cancellation, a helper, another accepted order). A short-notice order stays Incompatible. You can reject either one. Accept all and go to next day accepts the Requested ones and rejects any still Incompatible automatically.

5. Testing

Run python test_app.py. It never touches bakery.db: it works on a temporary copy that is deleted afterwards. It checks the reading of messages and dates. It also plays the whole week, Monday to Sunday, pressing Accept all and go to next day every day. It checks that no day ever needs more oven hours than it has, and that every order ends as worked out by hand.

Result of that run without helpers:

  • 24 orders taken, worth $3,640 in all ($3,000 for pickups Monday to Sunday, $640 for Mon 30 Nov and Tue 1 Dec).
  • 6 rejected automatically: Okafor and Adams for short notice; Park, Singh, Wilson and Bell because their day was full.
  • 2 cancelled.
  • Thursday, Friday and Saturday are full at 8 of 8 hours. No day ever goes over.

A second test hires a helper for Friday on Tuesday: Park's order then fits and is accepted, and Money shows the $100 cost. It also checks that a helper for Wednesday is refused on Tuesday (1 day's notice), and that Reject works.

To try the app by hand without touching your data, start it on a copy: python app.py --db test_copy.db.
